A long read

The rust-coloured bar at the very top of this page is ScrollFlow's progress bar — on by default, restyled here with one CSS variable. The chapter links below glide instead of jumping, because smoothScroll is on. Notice the address bar as you click: the links are ordinary anchors and ScrollFlow never touches them, so history and the back button behave normally.

Chapter one

Reading progress bars earn their place on long pages. A reader who can see the end approaching reads differently from one who cannot: the bar turns an unknown amount of scrolling into a known distance, and known distances feel shorter.

ScrollFlow draws its bar with transform: scaleX() rather than by changing its width, because changing a transform does not force the browser to lay the page out again. On a long article, that is the difference between a bar that glides and one that stutters on weaker phones.

The bar also knows when there is nothing to measure. On a page too short to scroll, the maths returns zero instead of dividing by zero — a small thing, until the day your landing page quietly grows a broken bar.

Chapter two

Smooth scrolling used to mean intercepting every click on every anchor link. That works, right up until a reader shares a link, presses back, or opens a link in a new tab and finds the browser's own behaviour replaced by yours.

The modern way is one line of CSS — scroll-behavior: smooth — and that is exactly what ScrollFlow's smoothScroll option applies, by adding a class to the page. Readers who prefer reduced motion get instant jumps instead, automatically.

Try the chapter links at the top again. Then press your browser's back button. It just works, because nothing was ever intercepted.

Chapter three

You are at the end, and the bar at the top should be full. That is the whole example: a progress bar, smooth anchors, and headings that reveal as they arrive — three attributes and two defaults, no configuration.

To turn the bar off, start the library with new ScrollFlow({ progressBar: false }) instead of using data-auto-init.
